# Player Roles and Archetypes

## Batting Roles

**Powerplay batter / opener.** Bats in overs 1-6 against the new ball and the field restrictions.
Judged mainly on strike rate in the powerplay and ability to survive the moving new ball. A high
career strike rate with a modest average is acceptable here.

**Anchor.** Bats top three, holds the innings together when wickets fall, and converts starts.
Judged on batting average as much as strike rate. An anchor with a strike rate under about 130 is a
liability in the current game unless the average is very high.

**Middle-order batter.** Bats at 4-6, normally arriving between overs 7 and 15 against spin and
the middle-overs squeeze. The key skill is scoring against spin without a set field. Look for a
strong record versus spin and a strike rate above 140.

**Finisher.** Bats at 5-7 and is asked to score at 10-12 an over in the last four overs, often from
ball one. Judged on death-overs strike rate, not average. Career average is a weak signal for
finishers because they are rarely not-out and rarely get long innings.

## Bowling Roles

**Powerplay bowler / new-ball bowler.** Bowls overs 1-6, takes wickets up front and controls the
run rate with field restrictions in place. Swing and seam movement matter more than raw pace.
Judged on powerplay economy and early wickets.

**Middle-overs bowler.** Usually a spinner bowling overs 7-15. The job is to squeeze and take
wickets through the middle. Wrist-spinners take more wickets; finger-spinners are more economical.
Judged on economy rate and strike rate together.

**Death bowler.** Bowls overs 16-20, the hardest job in the format. The required skills are the
yorker, the wide yorker, the slower ball and the bouncer, executed under pressure. Judged almost
entirely on **death-overs economy** and the ability to bowl at the stumps late. A death bowler
with an economy under about 9.0 across a career is genuinely valuable, and teams pay a premium for
them because there are very few. Common markers of a death bowler in this dataset are the tags
"Death bowling", "Yorkers" and "Slower balls".

## All-rounders

A **genuine all-rounder** bats in the top seven and bowls four overs. These are the most expensive
players in any auction because they let a team play an extra batter or an extra bowler.

A **bits-and-pieces all-rounder** or **sixth bowler** bats lower and bowls one to three overs. They
are useful as flexibility, not as a frontline option, and should not be paid all-rounder prices.

## Wicketkeepers

Every squad needs at least two keepers, one of whom is normally a frontline batter. A keeper who
bats in the top four is worth a large premium because the keeper slot is otherwise a cost to the
batting order. Overseas keeper-batters are common premium buys, but remember they occupy one of the
four overseas slots in the eleven.
