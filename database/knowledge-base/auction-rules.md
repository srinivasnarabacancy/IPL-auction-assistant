# IPL Auction Rules

## Purse and Team Budget

Each franchise is given a fixed salary purse for the auction. The reference purse used by this
assistant is **INR 120 crore** per team, and it is configurable per team in the budget tracker.
Money spent on retained players is deducted from the purse **before** the auction begins, so the
amount a team carries into the room is the remaining purse, not the full purse.

Every rupee committed at the auction counts against the purse for that season. A team cannot place
a bid that exceeds its remaining purse. Unspent purse does not roll over to the next season.

## Bidding Increments

Bidding moves in fixed increments that grow as the price rises:

- Up to INR 1 crore: increments of INR 5 lakh (0.05 crore)
- INR 1 crore to INR 2 crore: increments of INR 10 lakh (0.10 crore)
- INR 2 crore to INR 5 crore: increments of INR 20 lakh (0.20 crore)
- Above INR 5 crore: increments of INR 25 lakh (0.25 crore)

The auctioneer may vary the increment at their discretion when bidding slows down.

## Base Price

Every player enters the auction at a declared base price. A player cannot be sold below base price.
If no team bids, the player goes **unsold** and may re-enter in an accelerated round later in the
auction if a franchise nominates them.

## Right To Match (RTM)

A Right To Match card lets a player's previous franchise retain that player by matching the winning
bid after the hammer would otherwise fall. The number of RTM cards a team holds depends on how many
players it retained before the auction: the retention and RTM allowances together are capped, so
using more retentions leaves fewer RTM cards.

Under the current process, when an RTM is triggered the original bidding team is given one chance to
raise its bid once more; the RTM-holding franchise then decides whether to match that final number.

## Accelerated Rounds

After the main list is exhausted, unsold players and players not yet called can be nominated by
franchises into accelerated rounds. This is where teams with purse left over often fill squad gaps
cheaply, and where a team that overspent early finds it has no room to correct mistakes.
