# Auction Budget Strategy

## Purse Allocation

A common working split of a INR 120 crore purse:

- **60-70 percent** on the first-choice eleven's five or six key players (a top-order batter, a
  premium all-rounder, a keeper-batter, a strike bowler, a death bowler).
- **20-25 percent** on squad depth: the seventh to fourteenth players who will actually play games
  through injury and form.
- **10-15 percent** held back as reserve for the accelerated rounds and for uncapped Indian cover.

Teams that spend 90 percent of the purse on four players routinely finish the auction with an
unbalanced squad and no way to fix it.

## What Different Amounts of Remaining Purse Buy

These bands assume the standard market and are the bands this assistant reasons with:

- **Under INR 1 crore remaining:** uncapped Indian players and base-price fringe players only.
- **INR 1-3 crore remaining:** squad-depth players, uncapped Indian talent, a backup keeper, an
  overseas player past their peak, or a domestic bowler with a good season behind them.
- **INR 3-6 crore remaining:** a solid first-eleven squad player, a capped Indian bowler, a reliable
  middle-order batter, or a second-tier overseas all-rounder.
- **INR 6-12 crore remaining:** one frontline first-choice player in a single role, such as a
  quality death bowler, a top-order Indian batter, or an overseas strike bowler. At this level you
  are usually choosing one marquee need, not two.
- **INR 12-20 crore remaining:** a genuine marquee player plus a squad filler, or two solid
  first-eleven players if you avoid the bidding wars.
- **Over INR 20 crore remaining:** two marquee players, or one marquee plus a rebuilt spine.

## Cost Per Slot

Divide remaining purse by remaining slots to get the average you can pay per player. If you need 9
more players and have INR 12 crore, your average is about INR 1.3 crore per slot, which means at
most one player above INR 4 crore and the rest near base price. Checking this number before every
bid is the single most useful discipline in an auction.

## Common Mistakes

- **Chasing a marquee name into a bidding war** and leaving no purse for the bowling attack.
- **Buying a ninth overseas player** who cannot be selected.
- **Overpaying for a finisher's batting average** instead of looking at strike rate.
- **Ignoring the minimum squad size** and being forced to buy whoever is left in the accelerated
  rounds.
- **Not holding a reserve** for the accelerated rounds, where the best value of the whole auction
  is often available.
